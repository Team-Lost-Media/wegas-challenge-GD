Maltigi
Origin: LOST MEDIA (unknown) (popularized on Twitter)
Maltigi will prepare to dash towards you, picking a position and creating a red line between him and the target position. After a short delay he will quickly dash towards that position. [br] [br]Touching Maltigi immediately kills you.[br] [br]Maltigi's target position also takes your current velocity into account, attempting to predict your movement.
If you pay attention to him, Maltigi is not very big of a threat. Avoiding him is as easy as steering clear of the Maltigi Red Line, which is easy thanks to how quick your movement is. [br] [br]However, he can be dangerous once paired with more threatening enemies. Maltigi acts more like a support enemy: not dangerous by himself, but dangerous when paired with other enemies that pressure you, like Wega. [br] [br]Don't forget about Maltigi, and always avoid the Maltigi Red Line.
* Maltigi is really fucking evil. [br] [br]* He HATES Wega. He allies with him because both just wants to kill you. [br] [br]* Maltigi is frequently associated with LOST MEDIA [br] [br]* He is extremely homophobic. [br] [br]* He runs a scam call center.
Maltigi is my favourite enemy from WC:TI, so he was obviously going to be added, but implementing him was another story. [br] [br]First of all, I was CONVINCED he predicted the player's movement in WC:TI and decided to implement it. Doing so wasn't actually all that difficult, I just took the player's velocity and added it to his target position. However, in WC:TI he does not predict the player's movement; he overshoots his target instead. Implementing this was also rather easy (much easier than movement prediction, at least), but in the end I decided to stick to having him predict the player's movement, instead of overshooting the target. [br] [br]Instead, the most difficult part about coding Maltigi was what I call the Maltigi Red Line. The direction Maltigi will dash in is telegraphed by a red line. This is the Maltigi Red Line. And, I had no idea how to make it. The only solution I "found" (got told by someone in the #quick-help channel in the Godot discord server) was to draw the line using an "ImmediateMesh", which worked but under one condition: the line is really, REALLY thin, with no way to make it thicker. Better than nothing, sure, but I wouldn't consider it a fair compromise. That was until I discovered a Godot add-on called "DebugDraw3D", which just so happens to let you easily draw lines of varying thickness in 3D space! it also has absolutely no documentation god dammit [br]So, problem solved, right? With DebugDraw3D, I can have Maltigi draw the Maltigi Red Line at any thickness without issue! Well, that was until I continued development and tried to add Fried Maltigi to lap 2 (in the end, I scrapped him in favour of just making it regular Maltigi again). For whatever stupid reason, the add-on completely broke and drew Glitchigi's line at the default thickness, which is REALLY THIN. This was how the line was for the longest time until I messed around with the add-on for an hour (all with no documentation, mind you) and found the very VERY simple solution to everything hidden in one function that had almost the same name as what I was previously using. I hate the Maltigi Red Line.
