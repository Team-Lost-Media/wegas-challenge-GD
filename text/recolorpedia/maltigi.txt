Maltigi
Origin: LOST MEDIA (unknown) (popularized on Twitter)
Maltigi will prepare to dash towards you, picking a position and creating a red line between him and the target position. After a short delay he will quickly dash towards that position. [br] [br]Touching Maltigi immediately kills you.
As long as you know where he will dash, which is very easy thanks to the Maltigi Red Line, Maltigi is very easy to handle. Occasionally he could perfectly predict your movement, but you have plenty of time to react and it rarely happens in the first place.
* Maltigi is really fucking evil. [br] [br]* He HATES Wega. He allies with him because both just wants to kill you. [br] [br]* Maltigi is frequently associated with LOST MEDIA [br] [br]* He is extremely homophobic. [br] [br]* He runs a scam call center.
Maltigi is my favourite enemy from WC:TI, so he was obviously going to be added, but implementing him was another story. [br] [br]First of all, I was CONVINCED he predicted the player's movement in WC:TI and decided to implement it. Doing so wasn't actually all that difficult, I just took the player's velocity and added it to his target position. However, in WC:TI he does not predict the player's movement; he overshoots his target instead. Implementing this was also rather easy (much easier than movement prediction, at least.) [br] [br]Instead, the most difficult part about coding Maltigi was what I call the Maltigi Red Line. The direction Maltigi will dash in is telegraphed by a red line. This is the Maltigi Red Line. And, I had no idea how to make it. The only solution I "found" (got told by someone in the #quick-help channel in the Godot discord server) was to draw the line using an "ImmediateMesh", which worked but under one condition: the line is really, REALLY thin, with no way to make it thicker. Better than nothing, sure, but I wouldn't consider it a fair compromise. That was until I discovered a Godot add-on called "DebugDraw3D", which just so happens to let you easily draw lines of varying thickness in 3D space! it also has absolutely no documentation god dammit [br]So, problem solved, right? With DebugDraw3D, I can have Maltigi draw the Maltigi Red Line at any thickness without issue! Well, that was until I continued development and tried to add Glitchigi (also known as Fried Maltigi) to lap 2. For whatever stupid reason, the add-on completely broke and drew Glitchigi's line at the default thickness, which is REALLY THIN. This was how the line was for the longest time until I messed around with the add-on for an hour (all with no documentation, mind you) and found the very VERY simple solution to everything hidden in one function that had almost the same name as what I was previously using. I hate the Maltigi Red Line.
